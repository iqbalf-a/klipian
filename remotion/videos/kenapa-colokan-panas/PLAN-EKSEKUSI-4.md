## Diagnosis (from frames of the current render at 35.2 s, 40.6 s and the hook; code read in `src/components/Iso.tsx`, `src/cable.ts`, `videos/.../index.tsx`)

**1. The cable** (`IsoPlug`, `route`, `cablePath`)
- It is one constant-thickness tube along a few fixed points: a short stub out of the strain relief, then a hard hook, then a dead-straight horizontal run to the screen edge (the round-3 hook "leaves level to the left" made it straighter still). It reads as a bent pipe, not a cable.
- The route is a fixed list of points; it does not react to the plug. When the plug is pulled out (`gap`), rocks (`wobble`) or plugs back in, the cable keeps the same shape instead of following, dragging or falling on the floor. `cablePath` only sags a "long, mostly horizontal" stretch in screen space (about 3%).
- Only two tones (a dark body and one thin highlight), no contact shadow where it lies on the floor.

**2. The socket recess** (`IsoWallSocket`, the "hole or recess" recipe in DESIGN.md)
- The recess wall is one flat fill (`RECESS_SHADE`), drawn as a big crescent. The recess is deep (floor at -0.2, plate front at 0.3, radius 0.86), so the floor is shifted far under the hole and the wall crescent covers about half of the opening. The left pin hole is hidden under the crescent: only one dark hole and one small slot are visible (see 35.2 s and 40.6 s). That is why it looks like a bug.
- The floor tone (`ISO.plastic[1]`) is almost the same as the plate, so the recess has little contrast. The earth clips are plain metal-toned boxes whose faces do not follow the tone rule, so they look like loose white blocks. The scorch circles are drawn on the plate plane, so they ring the hole instead of reading as scorch on it.
- The tone rule (top light, +y mid, +x dark) is applied to extruded blocks but **not to the inside faces of the recess**: the wall should be graded by which way each part of it faces, not one flat grey.

**3. The yellow circle** (`index.tsx` lines 99 and 100)
- They are two **screen-space `<circle>` elements** at the socket's centre point: a growing ring and a steady ring r = 150. They ignore the wall's plane, so they never become the ellipse of the plate seen at an angle, and they are drawn over the plug (z-order wrong).
- They have no meaning on screen. The idea was "this is the new plate" (tip 2, "menggantinya"), but nothing says so.

## A. The cable (`src/cable.ts` or a new `src/cableSim.ts`, `Iso.tsx` `IsoPlug`, `plan.ts`)
Make the cable behave like a cable:
1. **A real shape**: a hanging cable of fixed length L (longer than the straight distance between its two ends, so it has slack), anchored at the plug's strain relief and at a point off screen or on the floor. Use a deterministic soft chain (about 24 nodes, verlet or a damped spring chain, fixed time step, gravity toward the floor) computed in `plan.ts` as a pure function of the frame (precompute the frames once and memoise; no randomness), then project the nodes with `isoAt` and draw them through `cablePath` (or a Catmull-Rom path) so there are no corners.
2. **It follows the plug**: moving the plug (`gap` 0 to 1.6 and back, `wobble`) moves the anchor, and the cable drags, swings and settles with a little delay and damping (no jitter, no overshoot loop). When the plug is out, the cable curls and rests on the floor with slack; when it goes back in, the slack is taken up.
3. **It leaves the plug along the plug's axis** for about the length of the strain relief, then bends smoothly (minimum bend radius about 0.45 world units, never a hook) and falls to the floor.
4. **Look**: three tones along the tube (a light stripe on the top side, mid body, dark underside, following the tone rule), tiny taper at the strain relief, and a soft contact shadow where it touches the floor (an ellipse under the lying part). Keep the width constant otherwise.
5. Remove the straight horizontal run: the hook scene must not end in a ruler-straight line to the edge of the frame. At least one gentle curve is visible in every frame where the cable is visible.
6. The story's other users of `IsoPlug` (AssetSheetIso, IsoDemo, the cutaway) keep working: keep the old props or give them a default route generated by the new chain.
7. Keep `cableTo` for explicit routes, but run them through the same smoothing and sag.

## B. The socket recess shading (`IsoWallSocket`)
1. Make the recess shallower: floor at about -0.05 (so the floor shift is at most about a third of the radius), so **both pin holes and the earth clips are fully visible** at the video's camera angle.
2. Draw the recess wall with the tone rule instead of one fill: build it as the inside of a cylinder (`cylinder(...)` with the faces turned inward, or a fan of thin quads) and take each quad's tone from the direction it faces (the side facing the light is lit, the opposite side dark), with a thin lighter rim line on the lip of the plate.
3. Raise the floor/plate contrast: the recess floor one tone darker than the plate face, the pin holes clearly darker with a small lighter rim, so the holes read at a glance.
4. The earth clips: small bent tabs fixed on the recess wall at the two sides, drawn with the metal tones by the tone rule, and clipped by the hole outline so they never poke out over the plate.
5. Scorch: draw it as a soft darkening of the plate face and the recess around the holes (an `OnPlane` ring with a radial gradient, plus the hole rims), not hard circles.
6. Check at 1:1 and at 3x zoom (the hook, 35 s and 40.5 s frames). Add a still of the empty socket and the socket with plug to `AssetSheetIso` so the look is reviewed on its own.

## C. The yellow circle (`index.tsx` lines 99 and 100, `plan.ts`)
Replace the two screen-space circles with something that has a meaning and lives on the wall:
1. **Meaning**: "this is the part to replace". Show it as a yellow **outline of the plate itself** (the plate's rounded-square outline, drawn on the plate plane with `OnPlane`/`planeXZ`, so it has the plate's perspective), with a soft yellow glow, together with a **number label with a leader line** (`NumberLabel`) whose text comes from the story (the tip 2 words, e.g. "stop kontak baru" if the story has it; otherwise the tip's own keyword). If no story text fits, skip the label and let the outline pulse once, but never leave an unexplained shape.
2. **Perspective and z-order**: the outline is part of the wall layer: drawn after the plate and the recess but **before the plug**, so the plug (which is in front of the plate) covers it where it overlaps. No ring is allowed in front of the plug.
3. **Animation**: when the plate is replaced, the old scorched plate swaps to a clean plate (a quick brightness pop, the outline sweeps once around the plate, then stays as a thin steady outline until the tip ends), instead of the growing circle.
4. Update the loop-back (round 3) so the outline is gone and the clean plate is the same as the opening plate at the last frame.

## D. Tests (`tests/colokan.test.ts`, plus a new test file for the cable chain)
- Cable: nodes are finite and the path has no corner (turning angle per segment below a limit); the length of the chain is constant (within 1%); it has slack when the plug is out; the first 0.3 units leave along the plug's axis; no ruler-straight stretch longer than 35% of the visible length; a frame sequence of the plug moving has a continuous cable (the largest node jump between frames below a limit); the first and last frames still match (the loop).
- Recess: both pin holes and both earth clips are inside the visible part of the floor circle (compute the floor circle's intersection with the hole at the camera angle); the recess wall tones follow the direction rule (the lit side lighter than the opposite side).
- Highlight: the outline's screen shape equals the plate outline projected with `planeXZ` (compare a few points), and the outline draws before the plug in the SVG order (check by index in the plan).
- Every existing check stays green; update the layout test if the new label needs a zone.

## F. Rules to add to `DESIGN.md` (ian: "put what follows from all the revisions into the rules")
Add each as one short, checkable rule in the right section (3 mascot, 5 cables, 2.5D isometrik, 8/9 for highlights, 10 quality bar, 12 handover):
1. **Mascot** (ian, round 3): small in the sting (about 200–240 px wide, no veil, the world keeps moving); the outro has no mascot and no text; a loop-back ending (state at the last frame equals the state at the first; periodic ambient motion; no music fade-out). *(Already written in round 3: keep and cross-check.)*
2. **Cables are never rigid**: a cable has a fixed length and slack, bends smoothly (minimum bend radius), leaves the plug along its axis, follows the plug when it moves (drag, settle, rest on the floor), has three tones and a contact shadow, and never ends in a straight run to the edge of the screen. It is simulated or solved deterministically per frame, not a fixed polyline.
3. **Holes, recesses and openings follow the tone rule on their inside faces**: the inner wall is graded by the direction each part faces, not one flat fill; the recess depth keeps the floor shift under about a third of the radius, so every hole and part inside stays visible at the video's camera angle; a hole has a darker fill and a lighter rim.
4. **Every highlight (ring, outline, arrow, glow) lives on the object it points at**: it uses the object's plane and perspective (`OnPlane`/`planeXZ`/`isoAt`), never a flat screen-space circle; it is drawn in the z-order of that object (an object in front covers it); it always has a visible meaning (a label with a leader line, or the word in the caption); an unexplained shape is a bug.
5. **A thing that is replaced shows the swap**: old part to new part (pop and outline), not just a circle.
6. **Review each close-up at 1:1 and at 3x**, and put the object on its own on the asset sheet; "looks like a bug" in a close-up fails the quality bar even if the layout test is green.
7. Quality bar, item 1: add "and every part of it is drawn correctly at the camera angle (no hidden holes, no floating pieces)".
8. Handover checklist (section 12): "highlights have a meaning and the right z-order; cables follow the plug; recesses show all their holes".
